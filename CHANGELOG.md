## [Unreleased]

### Fixed
- `dot`'s precision towards an input and `+`'s multivariate messages round as v6's did (Phase 7, item 6), found by running RxInferExamples models on both: the precision is v6's `v_a_vT(a, w)` again, `(a aᵀ) w`, exactly symmetric where the port's `(a w) aᵀ` was not always (FastCholesky warned "not symmetric" on an ARMA model), and a `Diagonal` for the AR package's standard basis vector, as in v6; `+` and `-` take a multivariate normal's moments with `mean_cov`, as v6 did. An ill-conditioned AR(2) had drifted from v6 by 1e-5 over 20 iterations; it is now bit-identical.
- `getdata(())` was ambiguous between the `Message` and the `Marginal` methods, the last method ambiguity in `ReactiveMP` (Phase 7, item 1). Aqua's `ambiguities` check, off since `main` had 322 pairs, is switched on.
- DiscreteTransition's corrections against v6 (Phase 6, step 9): the five-interface rule towards `T3` under belief propagation with a DirichletCollection `q(a)` normalised over `out` only (`softmax!(…; dims = 1)`), unlike every other rule of the node, and normalises globally now; four five-interface rules towards `T2` read their own edge's message and never matched, and the message towards `T2` is the generic rule's. v6 also failed on a Bernoulli `in` or `out` marginal, on the energy of a joint over three or more axes with a DirichletCollection `q(a)` (an ambiguity) and on that of a non-square `q(out, in)` with a point-mass `q(a)`, where the port computes what v6's generic method did. v6's point-mass rules clamped `A` to at most one, which only showed on a tensor that is not a probability tensor.
- `rule_inputs` is generated from its container's type: it built a `Vector{Any}` on every call, 336 bytes and 365 ns for one message, and a rule walking its pairs was type-unstable. `@define_factor_node` emits `interface_groups` as a literal, so the lookup folds.
- The Pólya nodes' energies and one of their rules (Phase 6, step 6):
  - BinomialPolya's average energy always took `softplus` at the mean of `xᵀβ`, the Monte Carlo estimate it computed being overwritten, and was biased low. It is the expectation under `q(β)` now, by Gauss–Hermite cubature (user, `DISCUSSION.md` §3.43).
  - MultinomialPolya's, for a Multinomial `q(x)` with N > 1, flipped the sign of `Σ ⟨log x_k!⟩` and took `log N` for `log N!`, and could be negative.
  - BinomialPolya's sampling path threw for a univariate `β` with more than one sample.
- ContinuousTransition's average energies (Phase 6, step 5). v6's were wrong in three terms: `⟨log det W⟩` was not halved, the dimension of the `log 2π` term was half of `q(y)`'s under mean-field or of `q(y, x)`'s under the structured factorisation, and the identity stood for `x`'s covariance beside the uncertainty of `a`. They were off by 1.1 to 2.4 nats in the cases checked, and v6's node test pinned its own formula. The closed form agrees with a Monte Carlo estimate to 0.5%.
- ContinuousTransition's rules towards `a` and `W` for an affine or nonlinear transformation (Phase 6, step 5; user, `DISCUSSION.md` §3.42): v6 took each row of `f(a)` as linear in `a` through the origin and dropped the offset `f(m_a) - J m_a`, which the node's other rules and its energy keep. Every rule now uses the same linearisation. Nothing changes for `reshape`; an affine `f` equals the linear model on `y - B x`, which the tests check exactly.
- `ARunsafe`'s joint `q(y, x)` for the AR and ConjugateAR nodes (Phase 6, step 4). v6's block inversion had a Schur complement of the wrong sign, and one transpose too many, so it disagreed with `ARsafe` even for a univariate AR(1), where `ARsafe` regularises nothing; for a multivariate AR it inverted the companion matrix, which is singular, and threw. The joint is now computed by the Kalman gain. It is exact, it agrees with a closed-form joint in the AR package's tests, and the v6 comparison declares the difference.
- The Probit node's average energy is finite for a wide `q(in)`: v6 computed `log(normcdf(x))`, which underflows at the far Gauss–Hermite points, and returned Inf, or NaN for a point-mass output (Phase 6, step 3). It computes `normlogcdf`.
- `CVIProjection`'s joint over several inputs converges (Phase 6, step 2, user): the inputs are projected in turn, each against samples of the others' latest projections, where v6 projected every input against the same samples of the previous proposal. On a posterior with two modes, such as `x * y` observed at 2, v6's inputs flipped between modes of opposite sign on successive calls, and its test that the proposal converges held only for its generator's stream; the proposal now settles on one mode. v6's convergence test is restored, and stricter.
- `DeltaApproximation` checks its method in every constructor (#11's follow-ups, Phase 6 step 2): the positional `DeltaApproximation(method, inverse)` skipped the guard, and the error named neither the methods the Delta node takes nor the package that provides one. It now lists `Unscented()`, `Linearization()` and `CVIProjection()` with ExponentialFamilyProjection, says v6's other methods are gone, and adds the method's own `delta_method_hint`.
- `NormalMixture` and `GammaMixture` check their components again, as v6's constructors did: at least two, as many means as precisions (shapes as rates), and a mean-field factorisation. Without the check, a mixture with three means and two precisions was accepted and its switch rule, zipping the two groups, silently dropped the third component.
- The engine distributes every `FactorizedCluster` that `check_factorized_cluster` accepts. It required the blocks, concatenated, to list the cluster's members in its order, so blocks in another order, or a block such as `(:a, :c)` of the cluster `(:a, :b, :c)`, were an error when a rule read them, though the public check accepted them. It now asks what that check asks: every member in exactly one block, each block in the cluster's order.
- `MessagePassingRulesTestUtils` runs a resolved rule under the algorithm the engine gives it, `rule_algorithm(spec, algorithm)`: a table, `verify_message_update_rule` or `test_rule_derivatives` run under a `DefaultAlgorithmExtension` now executes an inherited default rule, and preallocates its buffer, with `DefaultAlgorithm()`, as the engine does, rather than with the extension. A rule whose body dispatched on `::DefaultAlgorithm` failed under the tools and worked in the engine.
- `StandardMessagePassingRules`: the multivariate normals' average energies stay in their inputs' float type; `d * log2π` for an `Int` `d` made them Float64 whatever the inputs. They share a `gaussian_energy` helper, and a test checks every node with Float32 inputs.
- The `Gamma` and `GammaInverse` average energies in `StandardMessagePassingRules`, ported from v6 with v6's errors: E[x/θ] was taken as E[x]/E[θ] and E[θ/x] as θ/E[x], where they are E[x]·E[1/θ] and θ·E[1/x] (ReactiveMP.jl#672). The v6 node-test values that pinned them are replaced by hand-derived ones, and the v6 comparison declares both differences. In the engine, a `FactorizedCluster` whose blocks do not cover its cluster's members once each, in order, is now an error rather than a silent renaming; the helpers the generated `rule_marginals` calls are defined above it, so it also works without a package image. The free-energy test of a split cluster compared infinities and could not fail; it now checks the finite part and the count of infinities separately. Promotion checks are back on for three marginal-rule tables.
- `MessagePassingRulesBase`: `check_rule_ambiguities` reported disjoint rules as ambiguous. It intersected whole signatures, and when one input slot is disjoint the intersection is a valid but empty type such as `Messages{N, Union{}}` or `PointMass{Union{}}`, never `Union{}` itself. It now compares each rule's own method with Julia's `Base.isambiguous`, which ignores ambiguities that only a `Union{}` parameter could trigger. A method it cannot find is an error, never a skipped pair.
- Three claims in the design documents that did not survive checking against the code. The published `RuleSpec` measurement in `DISCUSSION.md` §3.14 could not distinguish the representations it compared, because a spec constructed inline in an inlinable `resolve` is constant-folded away and reports zero allocations for all of them. `PHASES.md`'s ambiguity table cited the arithmetic catch-alls as `rule.jl:358`/`:392`, which are the macro template's own lines — every one of the ~490 generated rule methods reports that same location, so `Method.file`/`line` cannot identify a rule at all. And the unexplained `reverse(...)` behind open item #6 lives in `normal_mixture.jl`/`gamma_mixture.jl` rather than `mixture.jl`, and is observationally inert: it reorders only the `combineLatest` trigger tuple while the payload comes from `map_to` un-reversed, so the regression to pin is scheduling, not values.
- Stale documentation in `docs/src/extra/contributing.md`: it referred to a `/demo/` directory that does not exist, to `make benchmark` and `make lint` targets that were never defined, and to `test_args` paths from a previous test layout. The `.PHONY` declaration in the `Makefile` also listed a `lint` target with no rule.

Its `ResolverError`-as-success stays, for Phase 8's strict downstream CI.
- **`investigations/message-type-parameter/`** records a performance investigation (Phase 7, the user's question; nothing applied): `Message{D}` against an untyped `data::Any`, with and without function barriers, and HEAD with two fixes. Dropping the parameter is 1.1–3.2× slower; barriers recover most of it, not all; and two costs in today's engine (a parametric message built from an `Any`-typed value, 510 ns per rule output, and callback events built with nobody listening, 270 ns per call), once fixed, make inference 4–63% faster. Scripts, diffs, raw results and the posteriors' equality check are kept for the end-of-refactor performance pass (`DISCUSSION.md` §5).
- **Log scales stay as v6 has them, past the release** (user, `DISCUSSION.md` §3.48): Phase 7's item 7 is closed with no change, no typed annotations, and no new requirement on rules; the brief below is kept as the survey. ## [6.5.0] - 2026-09-03

### Added
- A new exported `GaussianCoupling` factor node with potential `φ(out, in, a) = exp(out ⋅ a ⋅ in)`, the pairwise edge potential of Gaussian Belief Propagation ([Shental et al., arXiv:0810.1119](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.1119)), where `a = -A[i, j]`. Combined with `NormalWeightedMeanPrecision(b[i], A[i, i])` self-potentials it expresses the solution of `A x = b` as message passing. Adds the node definition (`@node GaussianCoupling Stochastic [out, in, a]`), the average energy, the `:out`/`:in` message rules, the `:out_in` joint marginal rule and a documentation page. Only the structured factorization `q(out, in) q(a)` is supported, which is applied automatically when `a` is a constant. The potential is not integrable on its own: the `:out`/`:in` messages are improper Gaussians with negative precision, and the joint marginal is proper only when `w_out ⋅ w_in > a²`. Per the paper, the inferred means are exact when `A` is strictly diagonally dominant (or `ρ(|I - A|) < 1`), but the inferred variances are exact only on acyclic graphs — on graphs with cycles they are walk-sum approximations of `diag(inv(A))`.

## [6.4.1] - 2026-08-18

### Changed
- The invalid-observation error added in 6.4.0 now names the third legitimate option instead of only two. It presented "a real number, an array of real numbers, or a `UniformScaling`" or `missing`, but a custom node may be defined over data that has no moments at all — text, symbols, a struct — and is fed by wrapping the value in a `PointMass` explicitly, which routes through the deliberately unvalidated `new_observation!(::DataVariable, ::PointMass)` method. That path was intentional from the start yet documented nowhere, so anyone with a genuinely non-numeric observation was told their model was wrong when it wasn't (this is exactly how the RxInfer "Large Language Models" example, whose rules dispatch on `PointMass{<:String}`, broke on 6.4.0). Also documented in the `new_observation!` docstring and in a new "Non-standard observations" section of the Variables page, cross-referenced from "Adding a custom node" and the inference lifecycle. Message text and documentation only; the accepted set of payloads is unchanged ([#588](https://github.com/ReactiveBayes/ReactiveMP.jl/issues/588))

- `LaplaceApproximation`'s `approximate_meancov` returned a **negated covariance for every input**, not just pathological ones. It computed `-cholinv(H)` from the Hessian `H` of the log-density at the mode. `-(H⁻¹)` and `(-H)⁻¹` agree in exact arithmetic, but `cholinv` factorizes via Cholesky, which is only defined for positive-definite input — and at a maximum `H` is negative-definite. Passing it directly does not raise; it silently returns a matrix that is not `H⁻¹`, so the sign flip was not compensated and the result was negative-definite. It now negates before inverting, giving `(-H)⁻¹`. Verified against the closed-form Gaussian-product answer, for which the Laplace approximation is exact. `LaplaceApproximation` had no test coverage at all, which is why this went unnoticed ([#635](https://github.com/ReactiveBayes/ReactiveMP.jl/issues/635))
- `LaplaceApproximation` now rejects a stationary point that is not a strict local maximum instead of returning an invalid Gaussian. `Optim.converged` only reports that the optimizer stopped moving, so for a non-log-concave integrand it could settle on a saddle point or flat region where the negated Hessian is not positive-definite. The error names the offending matrix and suggests approximation methods that do not assume local concavity ([#635](https://github.com/ReactiveBayes/ReactiveMP.jl/issues/635))
- A zero-covariance inbound message no longer crashes the `DeltaFn(:ins)` joint marginal rules. `unscented_statistics`' zero-covariance fallback returned `nothing` for the cross-covariance — "not computed" rather than a degenerate value of the same kind as the zeros beside it — and the marginal rules feed that third return value straight into `smoothRTS`, failing with `MethodError: no method matching *(::Nothing, ::Float64)`. It now returns a genuine zero cross-covariance, which is both correct (a deterministic input has zero covariance with anything) and type-stable with the non-degenerate path ([#630](https://github.com/ReactiveBayes/ReactiveMP.jl/issues/630))
- `smoothRTS` now short-circuits when the forward output covariance is singular or non-finite, returning the forward inbound statistics unchanged. A deterministic node output cannot be revised by the backward message, so that *is* the smoothed marginal. Previously `cholinv(V_tilde)` on a zero covariance returned `Inf` rather than raising, so `D_tilde = C_tilde * W_tilde` evaluated to `0 * Inf = NaN` and a silently corrupted marginal propagated. This affects both the `Unscented` and `Linearization` delta-node paths ([#630](https://github.com/ReactiveBayes/ReactiveMP.jl/issues/630))
- Documented the buffer-reuse contract of the multivariate `getpoints` generators for `GaussHermiteCubature` and `SphericalRadialCubature`. Both write every cubature point into a single preallocated vector and yield that same object each iteration, so `collect(getpoints(...))` returns one buffer repeated — silently wrong cubature with no error — and `map(copy, getpoints(...))` is required to materialize independent points. The reuse is intentional and load-bearing: `approximate_meancov` mutates the yielded point in place rather than allocating, so the sharing runs in both directions. Behaviour is unchanged; the contract is now stated in docstrings and pinned by tests ([#633](https://github.com/ReactiveBayes/ReactiveMP.jl/issues/633))
- Documented that `==` on `Message` and `Marginal` compares `data`, `is_clamped` and `is_initial` but deliberately **not** `annotations`, so two messages carrying the same distribution are equal even when their `:logscale` differs. Annotations describe how a message was computed rather than the belief it represents; callers needing annotation-sensitive equality should compare `getannotations` explicitly. Added to both docstrings with runnable doctests and to the message library page; behaviour is unchanged ([#632](https://github.com/ReactiveBayes/ReactiveMP.jl/issues/632))
- The `GCV` node's effective noise precision `⟨e^{-(κz+ω)}⟩` is now computed by summing the two log-exponents instead of multiplying `A = ⟨e^{-ω}⟩` by `B ≈ ⟨e^{-κz}⟩`. Either factor can overflow or underflow *on its own* while the product is perfectly representable, and the failure mode is a silent `NaN` rather than a large number: with `log A = +800` and `log B = -800`, `A = Inf` and `B = 0`, so `A · B` is `NaN` where the true value is `1`. That `NaN` reached the `:y`/`:x` messages, the `:y_x` joint marginal's precision matrix, and both `@average_energy` methods — i.e. a corrupted Bethe free energy. Values in ordinary ranges are unchanged (the full GCV suite passes identically). A variance that genuinely exceeds `floatmax` still returns `Inf`, which is the correct answer ([#624](https://github.com/ReactiveBayes/ReactiveMP.jl/issues/624))
- `InputArgumentsAnnotations` no longer records rule executions that never contributed to a message. `_merge_input_arguments` merged by `push!`/`append!`/`pushfirst!`-ing into one operand's `mappings` vector and returning that same object, but the operand is owned by a `Message` that `EqualityChain` caches and reuses across several all-but-one outbound products. The second product therefore saw a record that had already grown from the first, so the stored trace accumulated entries from unrelated products. Merging is now copy-on-write: it builds a fresh `ProductInputArgumentsRecord` and leaves both operands untouched. This affects only the diagnostic trace returned by `get_rule_input_arguments`, never the messages themselves, and only when `InputArgumentsAnnotations` is enabled ([#622](https://github.com/ReactiveBayes/ReactiveMP.jl/issues/622))
- A `missing` message no longer crashes when `LogScaleAnnotations` is enabled. `MessageMapping` short-circuits to `missing` without running the rule when any input is `missing` (the mechanism behind `new_observation!(datavar, missing)`), but it still invoked the post-rule annotation processors afterwards. `LogScaleAnnotations.post_rule_annotations!` only defaults `:logscale` to `0` when *all* inputs are `PointMass`, and otherwise calls `error(...)` — so a deferred message from a node with non-`PointMass` inputs was turned into a hard failure, defeating the `missing` short-circuit entirely. Post-rule processors are now skipped when the rule was short-circuited, leaving the message genuinely deferred and annotation-free. Products involving such a message were already handled correctly by `post_product_annotations!`'s `::Missing` dispatches, which copy the other side's annotations through unchanged ([#623](https://github.com/ReactiveBayes/ReactiveMP.jl/issues/623))
- The mean-field `GCV(:ω)` message rule (the `q_y`/`q_x` factorized method) was a copy-paste of the `:κ` rule and stamped `z`-dependent coefficients `(a, c, d) = (⟨z⟩, −⟨z⟩, Var(z))` onto the `ω` message. Since the `GCV` node is `p(y | x, z, κ, ω) = N(y | x, exp(κz + ω))`, `ω` enters the log-likelihood linearly with a *constant* coefficient of one and never inside the exponential's quadratic slot, so the correct coefficients are `(1, ψ·⟨e^{−κz}⟩, −1, 0)` — none of which depend on `z`. The bug silently corrupted the `ω` posterior, and everything downstream of it, whenever `GCV` ran under a mean-field factorization; it produced no error and no warning. The structured `q(y, x)` method and both `:κ` methods were and remain correct, so the two `:ω` methods disagreed with each other — an invariant now pinned by a regression test. `GCV` message rules previously had no test coverage at all, which is how this shipped undetected ([#621](https://github.com/ReactiveBayes/ReactiveMP.jl/issues/621))

## [6.3.0] - 2026-06-19

### Added

- `ConjugateAR` node for fully-conjugate variational inference of autoregressive models. It keeps the AR coefficients `θ` and the transition precision `γ` jointly on a single `MvNormalGamma`-distributed parameter edge `w` (rather than the mean-field `q(θ)q(γ)` split of `AR`). The `ConjugateAR(:w)` message implements the Normal-Gamma / Bayesian-linear-regression parameter update; the state messages (`:y`, `:x`), the `(y, x)` joint marginal, and the average energy delegate to the existing `AR` rules via the effective `(q_θ, q_γ)` moments ([#612](https://github.com/ReactiveBayes/ReactiveMP.jl/pull/612)).
